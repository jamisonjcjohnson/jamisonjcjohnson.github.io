DigitalLearn.org is a free online learning platform operated by the Public Library Association. It is designed to help people become more comfortable using computers, mobile devices, websites, email, and other everyday technologies.

Unlike platforms focused on programming or advanced technical certifications, DigitalLearn.org concentrates on practical digital skills. Its short lessons are especially useful for adults, seniors, job seekers, and anyone who has limited experience using technology.

In this blog post, we will review DigitalLearn.org and determine whether it is a good learning platform for people who want to improve their digital literacy.

## What Is DigitalLearn.org?

DigitalLearn.org provides self-paced courses covering basic computer and Internet skills.

Topics include:

- Using a Windows or Mac computer
- Navigating websites
- Searching the Internet
- Creating and using email
- Managing online accounts and passwords
- Recognizing online fraud and scams
- Using Android and iOS mobile devices
- Participating in video conferences
- Using Microsoft Word and Excel
- Creating resumes
- Searching and applying for jobs
- Accessing telehealth services
- Using cloud storage
- Understanding QR codes

The courses are available through the [DigitalLearn.org website](https://www.digitallearn.org/).

<br>

## Who Is DigitalLearn.org For?

DigitalLearn.org is best suited for:

- Adults learning to use computers
- Seniors becoming more comfortable with technology
- Job seekers developing workplace computer skills
- People using email or the Internet for the first time
- Library patrons attending digital literacy workshops
- English or Spanish-speaking learners
- Anyone who needs help with common online tasks

Older children and teenagers may also find some courses useful, particularly lessons about online safety, passwords, scams, and basic productivity tools.

However, the platform is primarily focused on practical digital literacy rather than entertainment, coding, or school-based computer science.

<br>

## Learning Experience

The courses are divided into short lessons that learners can complete at their own pace.

Each course clearly displays information such as:

- Difficulty level
- Estimated completion time
- Number of lessons
- Course category
- Learning objectives

Many lessons include narration, demonstrations, captions, and straightforward explanations.

Examples of beginner courses include:

- Getting Started on a Computer
- Navigating a Website
- Intro to Email
- Using a PC with Windows 11
- Using a Mobile Device
- Accounts and Passwords
- Online Frauds and Scams

The short format makes the platform less intimidating for learners who may not be comfortable completing a long online course.

<br>

## Updated Website Design

DigitalLearn.org received a major redesign in 2026.

The updated website includes:

- Mobile-friendly navigation
- Larger and more readable text
- Improved search
- Clear separation between courses and individual lessons
- Keyboard navigation
- Screen-reader support
- Adjustable text sizing
- Captions for video content
- Optional account-based progress tracking

The mobile-first design is particularly helpful for learners who use a smartphone as their primary Internet device.

<br>

## Does an Account Need to Be Created?

Most areas of DigitalLearn.org can be viewed without registering.

Creating an account can provide additional benefits such as:

- Saving course progress
- Creating a personalized course list
- Returning to unfinished lessons
- Tracking completed courses
- Receiving completion certificates where available

Users who only want to review a single lesson may not need an account.

According to the [DigitalLearn.org Privacy Policy](https://www.digitallearn.org/privacy-policy), account registration may require a name and email address. The website also uses analytics to understand how visitors use the platform.

<br>

## English and Spanish Courses

DigitalLearn.org provides courses and training materials in both English and Spanish.

This makes the platform useful for:

- Bilingual households
- English-language learners
- Spanish-speaking community members
- Libraries serving multilingual communities
- Trainers conducting bilingual workshops

Not every older course or training resource may be available in both languages, but many of the primary digital literacy courses include Spanish versions.

<br>

## Online Safety Lessons

DigitalLearn.org includes several useful courses related to online security.

Topics include:

- Creating safer passwords
- Managing online accounts
- Understanding Internet privacy
- Recognizing online scams
- Identifying fraudulent messages
- Using QR codes safely
- Protecting personal information

The online fraud and scam lessons are particularly valuable for older adults and new Internet users who may be unfamiliar with phishing, impersonation, and other common attacks.

These lessons provide a good starting point, although learners should continue following current security guidance because online scams change frequently.

<br>

## Job and Productivity Skills

The platform also includes practical employment and workplace lessons.

Available topics include:

- Creating a resume
- Searching for jobs online
- Completing online applications
- Preparing for interviews
- Using Microsoft Word
- Creating a basic Excel budget
- Using cloud storage
- Working with online documents

These courses will not replace advanced Microsoft Office training, but they can help beginners gain enough confidence to complete common employment-related tasks.

<br>

## Resources for Libraries and Trainers

DigitalLearn.org is closely connected with public libraries.

The separate [DigitalLearn Training website](https://training.digitallearn.org/) provides materials that libraries, volunteers, and instructors can use to conduct classes.

Resources may include:

- Instructor presentations
- Facilitator guides
- Lesson scripts
- Learner handouts
- Activity sheets
- Certificates
- Course-planning templates
- English and Spanish materials

This makes DigitalLearn.org more than an individual learning website. It can also serve as the foundation for in-person computer classes and one-on-one technology assistance.

<br>

## Advantages

DigitalLearn.org provides several important benefits:

- Courses are free.
- No previous technology experience is required.
- Lessons are short and beginner-friendly.
- Many courses are available in English and Spanish.
- The website works on computers and mobile devices.
- An account is optional for many activities.
- Online safety and scam-awareness topics are included.
- Job and productivity courses teach practical skills.
- Accessibility features support more learners.
- Libraries can download instructor and classroom materials.

The platform focuses on tasks that learners are likely to use in everyday life.

<br>

## Limitations

DigitalLearn.org also has some limitations:

- Most courses remain introductory.
- Advanced computer users may find the lessons too basic.
- Some software-specific courses can become outdated as applications change.
- The course library is smaller than major commercial learning platforms.
- There are limited advanced technical or certification courses.
- Learners may still need in-person help with difficult tasks.
- Completing a lesson does not guarantee that the learner can perform the task independently.

The platform works best when learners practice each skill outside the lesson rather than only watching the course.

<br>

## Best Way to Use DigitalLearn.org

A beginner can get started by:

- Taking the **Getting Started on a Computer** course.
- Learning how to use a mouse and keyboard.
- Completing the website navigation and Internet search lessons.
- Moving into email and account-management courses.
- Reviewing password, privacy, and scam-awareness lessons.
- Selecting job, productivity, or mobile-device courses based on personal needs.
- Practicing each task on an actual computer or mobile device.
- Visiting a local library when additional assistance is needed.

Learners should complete only a few lessons at a time and practice the skills before moving to the next topic.

<br>

## Overall Rating

**Rating: 9/10**

DigitalLearn.org is an excellent free resource for people who need help developing basic digital skills.

Its greatest strengths are its beginner-friendly language, short lessons, practical topics, mobile accessibility, bilingual content, and connection with public libraries.

The platform is not intended for advanced technical training. However, for adults, seniors, job seekers, and new computer users, it provides a strong and approachable starting point.

<br>

## Conclusion

DigitalLearn.org provides practical digital literacy training without requiring a paid subscription or previous computer experience.

Its courses cover many of the tasks people need for everyday life, including email, online searches, mobile devices, job applications, document creation, telehealth, password safety, and scam awareness.

The platform is particularly valuable when combined with practice, family assistance, or digital literacy support from a local public library.
